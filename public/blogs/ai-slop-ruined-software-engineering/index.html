AI slop ruined software engineering

This is the first post on my blog. I am Yash, a final-year computer science student who learned to code in the GPT era, and last week a dead phone taught me what I skipped.

I have been building apps for three years now, since my first year. Which makes me, officially, a programmer of the GPT era. I learned to code in the same years everyone else learned to prompt.

And I used to post every app I built on Reddit and X. Some of those posts just collected hate comments. Piles of them. It used to make me wonder why there was so much hate. Last week I finally got my answer.

Back in May my phone died. Not “low battery” died. Full reset, everything gone. Including Money Manager, where I had been tracking every rupee I spent. Months of expense data, wiped. And there was no way I was re-entering all of that by hand. Not happening.

So I thought: bank statements. Every transaction I have ever made is sitting in my banks’ records. Just pull those, turn them into a clean CSV, import it back into Money Manager, done. A parser, basically. Simple problem. Ancient problem. The kind of problem programmers were solving before I was born.

Except I have three bank accounts. Different banks, different kinds of expenses, different statement formats. Merging all of that by hand would take forever.

So obviously, GPT-era instinct kicked in: just dump everything into an AI and let it hand me back a Money Manager-compatible CSV. Thirty seconds, problem solved.

Then I stopped. This is my bank statement. Every rupee in, every rupee out: rent, food, the embarrassing 2 AM orders. Handing all of that to a cloud model so it can do date parsing felt wrong in a way I could not articulate at first. It is not that I distrust the model. It is that the tool is so wildly disproportionate to the task. Like hiring a Michelin chef to boil water. Plus, we all know what happens to that data.

Okay fine. Privacy matters. Run a local model then. I tried Qwen3 4B. Dude, that thing is so stupid. And on top of that it could not even read the PDF because the statement was locked. So now I needed to unlock a PDF just to feed it to a model that could not do the job anyway.

That is when I started actually looking for a solution. And I found it in some old Reddit post from 2010. No name I remember, no followers, no launch tweet. Just some guy with the exact same problem: bank transactions in, clean data out. He wrote a parser. I read through his whole post, then his GitHub repo. A few hundred lines. Ran in milliseconds, touched no network, sent nothing anywhere, and it would still work today.

I sat there for a minute. That was software engineering. Someone looked at a boring, unglamorous problem and solved it completely, with their own hands, in a way anyone could read and understand. No API key. No GPU. No per-token pricing. Just code.

Which made me wonder: is AI making us faster, or just lazy and stupid? I could have built this parser myself. No LLM, nothing. Pure rules and raw coding. An afternoon’s work, and it would have been done better than anything the models gave me.

Now I get the hate. Not the bitter kind. The mourning kind. The old guys were not gatekeeping. They were watching a generation skip the part where you learn how the machine actually works. And they were right to be sad about it. Because AI is not just getting smarter. It is making us dumber.

My CSV importer is going to be a parser. It is going to be boring. It is going to be perfect.

~ yash
